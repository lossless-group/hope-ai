# Goal pacing: leading indicators, paced to an outcome

The scoring philosophy behind the PSP toolkit, and the method the skill uses
to turn whatever a member says they want into a plan they can keep.

## The idea

You can *want* an outcome. You can't *do* one. Weight, revenue, a finished
album, a repaired relationship: these are **lagging indicators**. They move
late, noisily, and partly for reasons outside your control. Score yourself on
them week to week and a good week can look like failure.

What you can do are **process goals**, the **leading indicators** that
drive the outcome: fasting days, keto days, minutes in Zone 2, sales calls
made, evenings in the studio, calls to Mom. You control them, you can count
them this week, and when they're chosen well, the outcome follows.

So the plan has two layers:

| Layer | What it is | How it's treated |
|---|---|---|
| **Process goals** (leading) | Actions you take | **Scored** every week against a floor and a target |
| **Outcome** (lagging) | The result you want | **Read**, not scored: measured at Day 0, the midpoint, and the end (monthly for money) |

The outcome stays in the plan. It's the reason for the process goals, and
the readings are how you check the process is the right one. You just don't
grade a week on it.

## Two directions, one method

### When someone brings an outcome ("lose a lot of weight", "make $18k a month")

1. **Make the outcome concrete.** A number and a date: "20 lb by day 100",
   "$12k a month after tax by month 3". Ask, don't assume.
2. **Find the levers.** Which actions actually drive this outcome? Use what
   is known (model knowledge, and web research when it matters) rather than
   folk wisdom. For fat loss: food rules, fasting, daily movement, Zone 2,
   resistance work, sleep. For income: conversations, calls, asks,
   proposals.
3. **Pace the levers to the outcome.** Work out how much of each lever, at
   what weekly dose, plausibly gets there in the time available, using real
   rates. Show the arithmetic so the member can see why the numbers are what
   they are (the example plan's income math is the model: take-home →
   pre-tax → capital needed → conversations per week).
4. **Check the outcome is reasonable.** If the honest pace can't reach the
   outcome in 100 days, say so and offer either a smaller outcome or a longer
   horizon. Never raise the process dose past what's safe or sustainable to
   force the number.
5. **Score the process, read the outcome.** The weekly scorecard counts the
   levers. The outcome goes in a readings table.

### When someone brings a process goal ("walk every day", "call three people a week")

1. **Name the outcome it serves.** What do they hope changes? There may not
   be a measurable one (presence with family), and that's fine: say so.
2. **Project a reasonable outcome.** If they keep this process at this dose
   for 100 days, what does the evidence say they can expect? Give a range,
   not a promise: "kept up, this typically means roughly X to Y".
3. **Offer it as the reading.** That projection becomes the outcome they
   check at the midpoint and the end. If the readings lag the projection,
   that's information about the process, not a verdict on the person.

## Layer habits; don't stack them

The most common way these plans fail is ambition: too many new habits,
started all at once, at full dose. The toolkit's default is the opposite.

- **Start below what feels like enough.** Week 1's floor should be easy on
  the worst realistic week: travel, illness, a bad night.
- **Add one thing at a time.** A new habit joins once the previous one is
  holding, typically a week or two later. The weekly action plan is a ramp
  ("by end of week…"), each row adding to the standards already in force.
- **Ramp the dose, not just the count.** Intervals go 1 → 2 → 3 a week;
  sprints add one a week; an income target climbs across the quarter.
- **Count the total load across goals.** Five goals each asking for "just
  30 minutes a day" is 2.5 hours. Add it up and say so.
- **Deload on purpose.** Weeks 8 and 12 drop to the floors.
- **Put a ceiling on the over-doers.** For someone who tends to overwork,
  exceeding the ceiling (hours worked, editing time) counts as a miss, the
  same as missing the floor.

When a member arrives with an ambitious plan, the skill's job is to keep the
ambition in the *outcome* and make the *process* gentle enough to keep:
"Let's keep the 30 lb as the destination, and start with two habits you
could do on your worst day."

## Using real numbers, safely

Pacing depends on rates, so the rates have to be right.

- **Prefer evidence over memory.** If the skill can search the web, check
  rates that matter (safe weight-loss pace, training progressions, typical
  sales conversion) and name the source. If it can't, say the number is a
  general estimate.
- **Give ranges.** Bodies and markets vary. "0.5–1.5 lb a week" is honest;
  "1.2 lb a week" is false precision.
- **Be conservative with health.** Typical sustained fat loss is roughly
  0.5–2 lb per week (around 1% of body weight weekly at the aggressive
  end), and early drops on low-carb eating are largely water. Fasting,
  extreme diets, and hard training are things to clear with a doctor first;
  the example plan books a GP physical in Week 1 and lets the doctor's
  advice override the plan. Never pace a health goal past safe rates to hit
  a number.
- **Be conservative with money.** Revenue and capital outcomes depend on
  other people. Pace the asks and conversations; treat closes as readings.
- **Recalibrate at the midpoint.** If readings lag the projection, adjust
  the process or the projection, openly.

## Worked example: weight

Member: *"I want to lose a lot of weight. Like 30 pounds."*

1. **Concrete:** currently 210 lb, wants about 180; 100 days.
2. **Reasonable?** At a sustainable 1–1.5 lb a week, 14 weeks is roughly
   14–21 lb. Thirty is a six-to-seven-month destination. Offer: "20 lb by
   day 100 is ambitious but plausible, and the habits carry you to 30 after."
3. **Levers, paced and layered:**
   - Weeks 1–2: a 10-minute walk after lunch and dinner; no sugary drinks.
     (Floor: 10 of 14 walks.)
   - Week 3: add low-carb breakfast and lunch on 5 of 7 days.
   - Week 5: add two Zone 2 sessions of 30 minutes, then three from Week 7.
   - Week 6: add one longer fast a week, if the doctor agrees.
   - Weeks 8 and 12: deload to the floors.
4. **Scored weekly:** walks, low-carb days, Zone 2 sessions, fast done.
5. **Read at Day 0 / midpoint / end:** weight, waist, and (if they're
   getting labs) HbA1c. Midpoint expectation: roughly 8–11 lb down.

## Worked example: process first

Member: *"I want to call three people from my list every week."*

1. **Outcome it serves:** a stronger network; specifically, warm intros for
   their job search.
2. **Projection:** 3 calls a week for 13 weeks is about 40 conversations.
   If even one in eight turns into an intro, that's roughly five intros.
3. **Reading:** intros received, checked monthly. If it's zero at the
   midpoint, the process gets a look (who's on the list, what's asked),
   not the person.
